# Education philosophy — Astra Nova / Ad Astra reference

## What the source is

**Ad Astra** (Latin: *to the stars*) was founded in 2014 inside the SpaceX campus area, by Elon Musk together with Joshua Dahn, the teacher of his own children. It started as a home-schooling experiment for Musk's children and SpaceX employees' children — roughly a dozen students at first, capped near fifty.

Musk's stated reason: conventional schools were not producing what he thought education should produce. His two stated theses:

1. **Abandon the assembly-line model.** Grade levels are a blunt instrument — group by ability and interest instead, and let children of different ages work together.
2. **Focus on solving problems, not on tools.** Loading facts and methods into a child's head teaches neither problem-solving nor motivation.

Ad Astra closed as a physical school when Musk's own children graduated; the same team carried the model online as **Astra Nova** ("new star"). It now runs as a WASC-accredited online middle and high school, with a high-school division opened to applicants worldwide, and reports of students drawn from dozens of countries. A separate Montessori-inspired Ad Astra-style school for younger children is also reported in Texas, funded through Musk's charitable structure.

## The methods worth stealing

### 1. No grade levels — group by capability and interest
A child who shows aptitude in one area joins that group regardless of age. **Transferable, in modified form**: the newborn milestone table is a grade-level table. Replace "what should a 2-month-old do" with "what is this baby signalling right now".

### 2. Problems, not tools
Real questions drive the work: *who is responsible for the factory polluting the lake?* *How would you build a habitat on Mars?* *How should a pharmaceutical company decide under ethical pressure?*
**Transferable**: the infant equivalent is a real task, not a drill — sorting the drawer, hanging a sock, wiping the table.

### 3. Student-owned curriculum
A large share of topics each year is chosen by the students themselves; the curriculum is rewritten annually from what the previous projects and discussions actually taught.
**Transferable**: the baby repeatedly returns to one thing — that thing is next week's curriculum.

### 4. Teachers as practitioners, not credentialed teachers
Many instructors are working researchers and engineers from research labs and technology companies. *"First an expert in the field, then a teacher."* No teaching licence required.
**Transferable as a stance**: the adult's job is to keep the problem alive and safe, not to display the correct answer.

### 5. "Folio" — one deep topic at a time
A recurring weekly assignment in which a child researches a single subject in depth.
**Transferable**: a weekly note on what the baby returned to — kept **locally**, never in this project.

### 6. Judgement over scoring
Admission involves open-ended "Conundrums" with no standard answer — the school looks for *kind, curious and daring*, and for willingness to work hard and love learning, explicitly **not** for the highest IQ in the room.
**Transferable**: an infant cannot be scored. Read the behaviour.

### 7. Deliberate ambiguity
In the sibling game-based curriculum, the rules are **not** taught — children infer them, and the adult coach stays behind the screen offering indirect nudges rather than answers. The stated goal is to let children "make sense of things amid ambiguity and complexity, and come together to devise solutions".
**Transferable**: leave the problem unsolved; resist demonstrating twice.

## What does **not** transfer — and should not be copied

| Source feature | Why it does not transfer to 0-12 months |
|---|---|
| Student-chosen curriculum | A newborn cannot choose topics. The parent's role is to **read signals and supply the right problem**, not to hand over control. |
| Absence of adult guidance | At this age the adult is the **safety and scaffolding layer**. Removing adult scaffolding is not freedom, it is neglect. |
| Small online cohorts and long unsupervised sessions | Impossible and inappropriate for infants. |
| Mixed-age free grouping | Grouping by age is exactly what developmental screening uses. The "no grade level" idea is **not** a licence to ignore age in a medical context. |
| "Learn how you want to learn" | At 0-3 months, responsive interaction and consistent routine are the whole curriculum. |
| Ambiguity as a feature | Ambiguity is a teaching tool for older children. For infants, **predictability and responsiveness** are what build security. |

**Net**: the transferable core is a **stance about how adults interact** — read the learner, name the real problem, use the least tool, let them do it, write down what you learned. The institutional machinery around it does not transfer.

## Points where sources disagree

- **Non-profit or for-profit?** Coverage from 2021 describes the successor as a registered for-profit business with tuition scaled to family income and scholarships planned; coverage from 2026 describes it as a WASC-accredited non-profit online school. Both can be true of different entities or different dates — do not cite a single form as settled.
- **Founding and closing dates.** Most accounts place the physical school's operation at 2014-2020 and the online school's launch at 2020; at least one secondary write-up misprints the closing year. Treat the 2014 / 2020 split as the working assumption and re-verify before quoting dates.
- **Ages and reach.** Student age bands and enrolment counts have changed repeatedly as the programme expanded. Quote ranges, never a current headcount.

## The honest critique

- Fully online delivery means **few offline social experiences** — at least one family left for a conventional school explicitly seeking offline learning.
- The model has attracted **mixed reviews** from parents, including the view that some students felt they learned little.
- Education researchers have argued the interesting question is not the private school itself but whether its methods could lift **public** schools out of test-prep teaching.
- It remains a **single-maintainer, small-scale** experiment with a very different funding and staffing structure from ordinary schools.
