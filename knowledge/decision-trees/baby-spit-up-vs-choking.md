# Spit-up vs choking (newborn 0-3 months)

> ⚠️ **For reference only, does not constitute medical advice**. Follow doctor's guidance.
> **Skill positioning**: Separate "spit-up" and "choking" — two scenarios with **completely different severity** — to prevent parents from treating choking as spit-up.
> Not recorded: specific formula brands, supplements, lactation aids.

---

## Critical distinction (one sentence that saves lives)

| Type | Severity | Flow direction | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spit-up** (reflux) | 🟢 Physiologically common | Milk **slowly flows out of mouth corner**, baby shows no distress | Burp + recline + observe |
| **Choking** (aspiration) | 🔴 **Emergency** | Milk enters **trachea**, baby coughs / turns blue / breathing difficulty | **Immediate first aid + call 120** |

> **Core principle**: **First check "is the baby in distress" + "is breathing smooth"** — these 2 things are more accurate than any threshold.

---

## 5-step spit-up judgment (most common scenario)

1. **Timing**: during feeding / within 30 min after feeding
2. **Flow pattern**: slowly flows from **mouth corner**, small amount (a few ml to ~15 ml)
3. **Baby state**: no distress / not crying / smooth breathing / normal skin color
4. **Frequency**: 1-5 times/day is normal (**50%+ of 0-3 month babies have physiological spit-up**)
5. **Growth curve**: weight gain normal (this is the most important "OK indicator")

→ All 5 = physiological spit-up, **no medical visit needed**

---

## 5-step choking recognition (life-saving)

1. **Timing**: sudden onset during / after feeding
2. **Baby behavior**: **coughing** / wheezing / **face or lips turning blue** / can't cry out / breathing pauses
3. **Sound**: gurgling in throat / rapid breathing
4. **Consciousness**: alert but visibly distressed / or suddenly "floppy" (loss of muscle tone)
5. **Emesis**: may have blood / large milk volume gushes from nose and mouth

→ 1+ items hit = **immediate choking first aid + call 120**

---

## Choking first aid 4 steps (every new parent must memorize)

### Step 1: Head-down + back blows (within 0-3 seconds)

- Place baby **prone on your forearm**, head **lower than torso**
- Use heel of hand to **firmly strike back 5 times** (between shoulder blades)
- Purpose: use gravity + vibration to dislodge milk from airway

### Step 2: Flip + chest thrusts (3-6 seconds)

- Flip to **supine on your other forearm**, head still low
- With **index + middle fingers**, at **one finger-width below nipple line**, **press 5 times rapidly** (chest thrusts)
- Purpose: generate airflow to push out remaining milk

### Step 3: Repeat 1-2 until milk expelled

- Cycle "5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts" repeatedly
- **Until milk expelled + baby cries out + breathing smooth**

### Step 4: Seek medical care even after resolution

- **Must go to hospital** — choking can cause aspiration pneumonia (symptoms delayed 24-48h)
- **Don't just observe on your own**

---

## 7 red flags (see doctor immediately)

- 🟥 After choking, **face/lips stay blue > a few seconds**
- 🟥 After choking, **persistent rapid breathing / nasal flaring**
- 🟥 After choking, **worsening mental state / drowsy / feeding refusal**
- 🟥 Spit-up **with blood / green / coffee-ground color**
- 🟥 **Projectile** spit-up (shoots from nose > 50cm)
- 🟥 Weight **not gaining / losing**
- 🟥 Spit-up **with repeated coughing / wheezing** (suspect GERD)

## 5 common myths

- ❌ **"Spit-up = didn't burp"** — physiological spit-up is **not strongly related to burping**, just immature lower esophageal sphincter
- ❌ **"Spit-up = wrong formula"** — 50%+ healthy babies spit up; formula is rarely the cause
- ❌ **"After choking, hold upright and burp"** — **WRONG!** Choking first aid is **head-down prone + back blows**, **not** upright hold
- ❌ **"Place spitting-up baby on side to prevent suffocation"** — after feeding, **recline 15-20 min** is enough; **side-lying actually increases suffocation risk**
- ❌ **"Less spit-up = ate less"** — actual swallowed + absorbed amount is what matters; what's spit out doesn't count

---

## 5-step daily prevention (for spit-up, not choking)

1. **Don't overfeed** — 0-1 month 8-12 times/day is the rhythm, not "more = better"
2. **Burp between feeds** — when switching sides for breast; every 30-60 ml for formula
3. **Correct nipple hole size** — invert bottle, **1 drop per second** is ideal (too fast = choking risk)
4. **Recline 15-20 min after feed** — head 30° elevated, **don't** immediately lie flat
5. **Avoid post-feed position changes** — changing diapers / bathing / playing right after feed triggers spit-up
