# Early learning & play (0-3 months)

> ⚠️ **For reference only, does not constitute medical advice**. Follow doctor's guidance.
> **Skill positioning**: A decision framework for "what do I do with my baby right now to support learning" — built on the project-based / interest-first / no-grade-level principles pioneered by Astra Nova (see `references/education-philosophy.md`), adapted to the 0-3 month window.
> Not recorded: early-education course brand names, toy brand names, month-by-month screen-time thresholds, milestone score tables (pediatric guidance sources have more).

## First: is this learning, or is this a problem?

Before any play, screen out medical and developmental issues. A parent applying "follow the baby's interest" to a baby who is failing to track, floppy, or not gaining weight has skipped the most important step.

1. **Alertness / state**: awake and responsive to a face? Or hard to wake, floppy, or unusually irritable?
2. **Social response**: does the baby look back, coo, and show social interest in a familiar face?
3. **Movement**: head control improving in tummy time? Symmetrical use of both sides of the body?
4. **Oral / feeding**: feeding and swallowing coordinated, no choking episodes (see spit-up-vs-choking tree)?
5. **Growth**: weight trending along its own curve (see feeding-rhythm tree)?

→ Any red flag below = **medical path, not the play path**

---

## 7 red flags (professional assessment, not home play)

- 🟥 **No social smile and no eye contact** by roughly 8 weeks of age
- 🟥 **Does not startle or turn toward sound** at any age
- 🟥 **Does not follow a slowly moving object** with the eyes across the room
- 🟥 **Floppy tone** (limp, "rag doll") or **persistent stiffness** in arms or legs
- 🟥 **Strong head lag** when pulled to sitting — no head control effort
- 🟥 **One side of the body clearly used less** than the other
- 🟥 **Weight faltering or crossing down percentile bands** — this is a feeding/medical issue first

→ Any one item = **talk to a pediatrician or a developmental professional**
→ "Interest-led free play" never overrides a red flag

## 5-step interest-first play method

This is the infant version of the Astra Nova loop: *read the learner → name the real problem → use the least tool → let them do it → write down what you learned*.

### Step 1 — Read the signal, not the age chart
Look at what the baby is doing **in this moment**: reaching, mouthing, staring, crying, going still.
This replaces the milestone table. The chart tells you what a *population* does; the signal tells you what **this** baby is working on.

### Step 2 — Name the real problem
Not "what shall I teach" but "what is the actual problem in front of me":
- Slightly hungry → the problem is feeding rhythm, not cognition
- Overstimulated by a noisy room → the problem is environment, not a lesson
- Grips a cloth and won't let go → **the problem is grasping and persistence — that IS the lesson today**

Real household tasks beat toy drills: dressing, wiping the table, sorting the drawer, hanging a sock. This is the "problems, not tools" rule.

### Step 3 — Use the least tool
Use what is already in the room before buying anything. The baby does not need a flashcard to learn; they need a surface, a texture, a sound, and you.
Fewer, more open-ended materials → more problem-solving per object.

### Step 4 — Let them do it — do not correct, do not demonstrate twice
The adult's job is to keep the problem alive and safe, not to show the "right" answer.
Repeat the same activity for days. Repetition is the project; novelty is not the goal.

### Step 5 — Write one page of Folio (keep it local)
Once a week, note **what the baby returned to** and what they did with it. That note stays in the user's local notes — it does not enter this project (see privacy iron rule).
Next week, **continue the thing they returned to**. That is the whole curriculum-planning method: design the next unit from the learner's own signals, not from a syllabus.

## 5 common myths

- ❌ **"The milestone chart is a target to hit"** — it is a *screening* instrument, not a scoreboard. One item later than the line means nothing on its own; the pattern and the trajectory are what matter.
- ❌ **"Earlier classes and flashcards = stronger development"** — in this age window, group early-education classes add little over responsive play and add infection exposure. Nothing about a 2-month-old needs a syllabus.
- ❟ **"Screen time helps attention"** — screen exposure in early infancy shows no learning benefit; for this window, live talk and face-to-face interaction is the effective channel, and public pediatric guidance recommends against screen media this young (except live video chat).
- ❌ **"If they don't focus on my activity, they are behind"** — infant attention is short, and brief attention is normal. What matters is **responsive return**: they signal, you respond, they signal again.
- ❌ **"An AI answer about infant development can be followed as-is"** — it cannot. It is unverified for this domain. Use it as a *framework to think with* (like this file), never as an instruction set, and let the red flags override it.
