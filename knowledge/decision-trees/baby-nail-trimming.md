# Newborn nail trimming (0-3 months)

> ⚠️ **For reference only, does not constitute medical advice**. Follow doctor's guidance.
> **Skill positioning**: Only record the framework (**when to trim, how to trim, why scratch prevention**) and **common pitfalls**.
> Not recorded: specific nail clipper brands, baby mittens brands.

---

## Why newborn nails need attention

- Newborn nails grow fast (~0.1 mm/day)
- Nails are thin and sharp, **can scratch baby's own face** (especially the cheeks, eyes)
- Babies can't control their hands, **frequent face-scratching is normal**
- **Don't wear mittens** (affects tactile development) — **trim nails instead**

---

## 5-step trimming framework

1. **Best timing**: **after a bath** (nails softest) or **during deep sleep** (hands relaxed)
2. **Lighting**: bright / natural light, **can see the nail edge clearly**
3. **Tool**: **baby-specific nail clippers** (small blade, curved edge) or **baby nail scissors** (rounded tip)
4. **Posture**: hold baby's hand firmly, **press fingertip pad away from nail** (avoid cutting skin)
5. **Cut shape**: **straight across**, not curved into the sides (prevents ingrown nails)

> **Frequency**: ~once a week for fingernails, ~once every 2 weeks for toenails

---

## 5 red flags (stop trimming + see doctor)

- 🟥 **Cut skin** while trimming (small amount = OK, press to stop bleeding)
- 🟥 **Paronychia signs** (red / swollen / pus around nail) → infection, see doctor
- 🟥 **Nail discoloration** (yellow / black / striped) → could indicate fungal or systemic issue
- 🟥 **Ingrown nail** (nail growing into skin) → see pediatrician, don't self-treat
- 🟥 **Bleeding won't stop after 5 min of pressure** → could indicate coagulation issue

---

## 5 common myths

- ❌ **"Mittens prevent scratching"** — **wrong**: long-term mittens affect tactile development; **trim nails instead** is the only correct approach
- ❌ **"Bite nails off with mouth"** — **unsanitary** + adult mouth bacteria harmful to baby
- ❌ **"Trim when baby is awake and active"** — **wrong**: hands flailing = cut risk; trim during **deep sleep or after bath**
- ❌ **"Cut very short to extend interval"** — **wrong**: cutting too short exposes nail bed, painful + infection risk
- ❌ **"Trim in a curve to match the nail shape"** — **wrong**: curved cuts cause ingrown nails; **straight across only**

---

## 5-step scratch-prevention alternatives (if you really can't trim)

1. **Use sleeves** that cover hands (only when absolutely needed, not daily)
2. **File nails with emery board** instead of cutting (safer for new parents, slower)
3. **Trim during feed** — baby focused on feeding, hands more relaxed (less ideal than sleep, but workable)
4. **Have partner hold baby's hand** while you trim (4 hands safer than 2)
5. **Wait until baby is deeply asleep** (eyes still, breathing slow) — **best window**

> **If you really cannot trim safely**: filing is the safest alternative. Better slightly long than accidentally cut the baby.

---

## 5-step if you accidentally cut the baby

1. **Don't panic** — small nicks are common
2. **Press clean gauze / cotton ball firmly** for 5 min
3. **Check if bleeding stopped** — if yes, no need for further action
4. **If bleeding won't stop in 5 min** = see doctor (coagulation check)
5. **Don't apply "stop-bleeding powder"** (most contain ingredients not for newborns)

> **If cut area gets red / swollen / pus in 24-48h** = paronychia, see pediatrician
