# Newborn umbilical cord care (0-4 weeks)

> ⚠️ **For reference only, does not constitute medical advice**. Follow doctor's guidance.
> **Skill positioning**: Only record the framework (**dry care method, when stump falls off, infection signs**) and **common pitfalls**.
> Not recorded: specific antiseptic brands, umbilical belts, cord storage / banking services (commercial).

---

## Basic framework

### Normal timeline

| Stage | Time | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Newborn | Day 0-1 | Stump is white/blue, moist, clamped |
| 2. Drying | Day 2-7 | Stump turns brown/black, shrinks, dry |
| 3. Separating | Day 7-14 | Base shows separation, may have mild smell |
| 4. Falling off | Day 10-21 | Stump detaches, small raw area left |
| 5. Healing | Day 14-28 | Navel fully closed, dry |

> **Normal range**: stump falls off **between 1-4 weeks**. **> 4 weeks still attached = see doctor**.

---

## 5-step dry care method (current standard)

> **Modern recommendation (AAP / WHO)**: **"Dry care" — keep clean and dry, no alcohol, no bandages**. This replaced the old "alcohol swab" method.

1. **Keep clean** — sponge bath only (no tub bath) until stump falls off + 1-2 days after
2. **Keep dry** — fold diaper down below the navel (expose stump to air)
3. **Air exposure** — wear loose clothing / onesie, avoid tight diapers covering navel
4. **No pulling** — **never pull the stump** even if it looks "almost off" (let it fall naturally)
5. **No antiseptic** (most cases) — unless doctor specifically prescribed (e.g. premature / infection risk)

> **If doctor prescribed chlorhexidine / alcohol** (e.g. NICU baby / high infection risk area): use as directed, but for most healthy full-term babies, **dry care is enough**

---

## 5 red flags (immediate medical visit)

- 🟥 **Foul smell** from the stump (not mild musty, but distinctly bad)
- 🟥 **Yellow / green pus** discharge
- 🟥 **Redness spreading** from navel to surrounding skin (> 1 cm)
- 🟥 **Fever ≥ 38°C** + navel abnormal (any combo)
- 🟥 **Bleeding that won't stop** with 5 min pressure (small blood spots when falling off = normal)

---

## 5 common myths

- ❌ **"Apply alcohol to dry the stump faster"** — **old advice, now wrong**; alcohol delays drying, can cause irritation
- ❌ **"Wrap coin / bandage to prevent hernia"** — **wrong**: doesn't prevent umbilical hernia, increases infection risk
- ❌ **"Pull the stump when almost off"** — **wrong**: pulling causes bleeding + infection; let it fall naturally
- ❌ **"Don't bathe until 1 month"** — **wrong**: **sponge bath is fine**; only avoid tub/submerging the navel
- ❌ **"Cord blood banking is necessary"** — commercial decision, not medical necessity; this Skill does not recommend

---

## 5-step daily care routine

1. **Each diaper change**: fold diaper below navel (expose to air)
2. **If stump gets dirty** (urine / stool): clean with **plain water + soft cloth**, pat dry
3. **Don't use alcohol / hydrogen peroxide / antibiotic ointment** (unless doctor prescribed)
4. **After stump falls off**: small amount of blood-tinged discharge is normal for 1-2 days
5. **Watch for healing**: navel should be dry and closed by 4 weeks; if not, see doctor

---

## 5 things NOT to do

1. **Don't submerge in water** (no tub bath / swimming until healed)
2. **Don't apply traditional remedies** (herbs / oils / powders) — unsterile, infection risk
3. **Don't use belly bands / umbilical belts** — outdated practice, no benefit
4. **Don't pick at scab** — let it fall naturally
5. **Don't ignore spreading redness** — early treatment of omphalitis prevents sepsis

---

## 5-step after stump falls off

1. **Continue dry care** for 1-2 days
2. **Small blood spot or moistness** is normal
3. **Clean with water if dirty**, pat dry
4. **If small granuloma forms** (pink moist bump): see doctor, may need silver nitrate treatment
5. **Full healing typically 2-3 weeks after stump falls off**
